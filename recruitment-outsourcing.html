Recruitment Outsourcing in Nigeria: What Employers Should Know

Recruitment outsourcing can help businesses manage hiring demands without asking internal teams to handle every stage of recruitment alone.

Recruitment involves more than advertising a vacancy. Employers need to define the role, attract applicants, screen candidates, coordinate interviews and communicate decisions. When hiring volumes increase, these tasks can put pressure on managers and internal HR teams.

Recruitment outsourcing involves engaging an external provider to carry out some or all agreed recruitment activities. The appropriate arrangement depends on the roles, hiring volume, internal capacity and level of support required.

What can recruitment outsourcing include?

The scope can range from support for one vacancy to a coordinated process across multiple roles. Activities may include:

  • Clarifying job requirements and candidate criteria.
  • Sourcing applicants through suitable channels and candidate databases.
  • Screening applications against agreed requirements.
  • Preparing shortlists and coordinating interviews.
  • Maintaining candidate communication and recruitment records.
  • Providing progress updates and agreed recruitment metrics.

Before recruitment begins, clarify who will interview candidates, approve offers, communicate decisions and manage employment documentation and onboarding.

When should a business consider outsourcing?

Outsourcing may be useful when several vacancies need to be filled at once, hiring deadlines are tight, specialist skills are difficult to source or the internal team has limited capacity. It may also help establish a more consistent process when departments recruit differently.

However, outsourcing is not automatically the best option for every vacancy. Consider the complexity of the role, expected workload, service cost and the organisation's ability to manage recruitment internally.

How to prepare before engaging a provider

1. Define the requirement

Confirm the responsibilities, location, employment type, essential criteria, reporting line and expected start date. Provide a realistic salary range where possible. A clear brief helps the provider identify candidates who are genuinely suitable.

2. Agree the recruitment process

Decide how applicants will be screened, who will interview candidates, how feedback will be shared and who has final hiring authority. Agree how often progress updates will be provided.

3. Put the service scope in writing

Document deliverables, timelines, fees, responsibilities, confidentiality, candidate data handling and any applicable replacement terms. Both parties should understand what is included and what would attract additional charges.

4. Protect the candidate experience

Agree who will update applicants, how quickly feedback should be provided and how candidate information will be handled. Timely communication reflects on both the employer and the recruitment provider.

How should recruitment results be measured?

Choose measures that reflect the agreed scope and business needs. Useful indicators may include:

  • Time taken to present a suitable shortlist.
  • Number of candidates meeting the agreed criteria.
  • Interview-to-offer and offer-acceptance rates.
  • Hiring manager satisfaction with candidate quality.
  • Retention after an agreed period, where measurable.

Interpret the measures together. Speed alone does not demonstrate recruitment quality, and results can be affected by salary competitiveness, slow feedback or changes to the original role requirements.

Questions to ask a recruitment partner

  • What types of roles and sectors have you recruited for?
  • How do you source and assess candidates?
  • What progress reports will we receive?
  • How do you protect candidate and employer information?
  • What are the fees, exclusions and responsibilities of each party?
  • How will you respond if the shortlist does not meet the brief?

Choosing the right approach

A productive outsourcing arrangement starts with a clear brief, realistic expectations and an agreed process. The provider should understand the employer's needs, communicate progress and present candidates against agreed criteria. The employer should provide timely feedback and retain responsibility for its hiring decisions.
