HR for SMEs in Nigeria: A Practical Guide

Small businesses need clear, consistent people practices that support hiring, employee management and sustainable growth.

For a small or growing business, people management can quickly become a business priority. Unclear responsibilities, inconsistent workplace rules and unreliable employee records can consume management time and create avoidable problems.

A practical HR foundation does not have to be complicated. It should help employees understand expectations, help managers make consistent decisions and keep essential processes organised.

1. Define roles before recruiting

Before advertising a vacancy, clarify why the role is needed, its main responsibilities, reporting line and expected results. A job description helps applicants understand the opportunity and gives the business a consistent basis for screening and interviews.

Use job-related criteria when assessing applicants. Document the recruitment process and communicate clearly with candidates throughout it.

2. Put essential workplace policies in writing

Employees and managers should be able to find the organisation's basic rules without relying on memory or informal messages. Depending on the business, these may cover attendance, working hours, leave, conduct, grievances, discipline, confidentiality and the use of company equipment.

Policies should reflect how the business operates and applicable Nigerian requirements. Avoid copying another organisation's policies without checking whether they are suitable for your workforce.

3. Maintain accurate employee and payroll records

Keep appropriate records of employment terms, employee details, leave, attendance and relevant employment decisions. Restrict access to personal information and store it securely.

Payroll should follow a defined process for validating changes, checking calculations, obtaining approval and reconciling payments. Confirm current statutory requirements with a competent adviser where necessary.

4. Set expectations and review performance

Employees work more effectively when they understand their priorities and how success will be assessed. Agree clear objectives, review progress regularly and document important feedback and agreed actions.

Performance management should not be limited to an annual appraisal form. Regular conversations can identify obstacles early, recognise progress and clarify where additional support is needed.

5. Handle employee concerns consistently

Provide a clear way for employees to raise concerns. Managers should listen, document relevant information, maintain appropriate confidentiality and follow established procedures. Sensitive employee relations matters may require specialist HR or legal advice.

Consistency matters: similar situations should be handled using the same principles, while still considering the facts of each case.

6. Decide what to manage internally

An SME may manage everyday people matters internally while seeking external support for recruitment, policy development, payroll administration, training or complex employee relations issues.

The right arrangement depends on workforce size, internal capability, business risks and budget. Before engaging a provider, agree the scope, deliverables, responsibilities, fees and how success will be assessed.

A practical HR checklist for SMEs

  • Clear job descriptions and reporting lines.
  • Written employment terms and essential workplace policies.
  • Accurate and appropriately protected employee records.
  • A documented payroll review and approval process.
  • Clear objectives and regular performance conversations.
  • A consistent process for employee concerns.

When should a business seek external HR support?

Consider external support when hiring consumes too much management time, workplace practices differ between teams, employee issues become difficult to manage or the business is preparing for growth. An external adviser can help assess the current position and identify practical improvements.
